Relationship Satisfaction Predicts Life Satisfaction Independent of Questionnaire Order: A Preregistered Test of the Focusing Illusion

Nissel, N., & Enko, J.

Free PDF (accepted manuscript) Published version · DOI

Abstract

The focusing illusion suggests that making a life domain salient can inflate its weight in global life satisfaction judgments. However, order effects may be weaker for important domains whose information is already chronically accessible. We conducted a preregistered between-subjects experiment (N = 478) to test whether questionnaire order moderates the association between relationship satisfaction and life satisfaction. Participants completed the Satisfaction With Life Scale and the Couple Satisfaction Index in counterbalanced order. Relationship satisfaction was a strong positive predictor of life satisfaction; however, this association did not vary by questionnaire order, and no gender moderation emerged. Bayesian model comparison favoured a model including relationship satisfaction without interaction terms. These findings suggest that the association between romantic relationship satisfaction and life satisfaction did not depend on questionnaire order in the present study.

Keywords: life satisfaction, relationship satisfaction, focusing illusion, gender differences, well-being
